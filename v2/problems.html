The research, laid out plainly

42 real problems. Sourced, not guessed at.

This started as a question: what's actually going wrong for the people we build this for? So we went and looked — industry studies, response-time research, freelancer and HR surveys, documented cases. Each one below opens into a full breakdown: why it happens, what it costs, why the usual fixes fail, and what a working setup looks like.

Website & Presence Marketing & Visibility Leads & Follow-Up Sales & Closing Programs & Structure Community & Marketplaces

How this was put together: every account is a realistic composite written to show the pattern clearly, not a verbatim quote pulled from one person's post, since we don't have that kind of platform access set up and won't pretend otherwise. Every statistic is a different matter: each is checked against its original source, and anything we couldn't independently verify was cut rather than kept for effect. If you check a source and find we got it wrong, tell us. This page only works if it's actually true.

Where CoachOS fits in: each breakdown ends with one plain note on what changes when this is handled for you, and by whom. Our AI systems draft and monitor the repetitive parts; a specialist on our team reviews before anything reaches you or a client. Not an autonomous AI running unsupervised.

Website & Presence

What's true if the only proof you exist is a social feed

17%

No real website, so a prospect can't verify you're legitimate

Someone asked for my website on a Tuesday and I sent them my Instagram. She said thanks, that's great, and then just — nothing. I'd been telling myself the site could wait because everything came through referrals anyway, and it did, mostly. It's the ones that don't come through that you never hear about. The domain's been sitting in a cart since March.

A slow site loses people before they see what is on it

We ran ads for three weeks and I kept checking the numbers on my phone at night, wondering why the clicks weren't turning into anything. Turned out the homepage took about nine seconds on a phone. On my laptop in the office it was instant, which is exactly why I never caught it — I'd only ever looked at it there. There was a video header nobody asked for and a hero photo that was 8MB. So we paid for a few hundred people to look at a white screen.

The renewal notices had been going to an address on that same domain, which — yeah. Card on file had expired in January and I never updated it. We got it back on the Thursday but the ten days of bounced enquiries are just gone.

Nothing dramatic happened — I signed two clients in April and just stopped, and I didn't notice because enquiries kept trickling in for a while off older stuff. They've stopped now. So I'm posting again and it's like the first month all over again.

I do client work during the day and the marketing after nine at night, so the marketing gets whatever's left of me. That was fine for about eight months. Then I missed the Friday newsletter twice, and after that I stopped writing captions properly and just posted the photo. I wouldn't say I burned out, exactly. I just went quieter and quieter until there wasn't much left going out.

Leads & Follow-Up

What the research says actually happens to an inbound lead

$27.66

The same ad budget buys roughly half the leads it used to

We were sitting at about £11 a lead in March. I checked on a Sunday night because I couldn't sleep and it was £26. Same ad, same audience — I actually went into the version history to see whether I'd changed something and forgotten about it. Now the same money brings in maybe half the enquiries and honestly a lot of them are worse. I keep meaning to sit down properly and work out whether it still adds up.

A purchased lead is often sold to several competitors at once

Bought a batch of them, forty quid each. Rang the first one at ten past eight on a Tuesday and the bloke said I was the third that morning — one of the others had already been out to look at the job. I did try the refund thing; you've got 48 hours and they want call recordings, and I had recordings for about half. Ended up quoting low on two just so the month wasn't a total write-off. Still buying them, which probably says something.</p>

The annoying part is that I know precisely why it happened and I'll probably do it again.

$244/hr

Years of mentoring, and still no real answer when someone asks what you offer

A woman at my nephew's christening asked what I do and I gave her about forty seconds of something before I heard myself say "it depends". Eleven years I've been doing this. I can tell you exactly what happened with any one of them — the guy who moved out of sales into product, the woman who finally left her firm — but I can't tell you what the thing is that I sell. My rate hasn't changed since 2019 because I wouldn't know what to say to justify changing it.
