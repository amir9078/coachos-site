The research, in their words

This started as a question: what’s going wrong for the people we build this for? So we went and looked — industry studies, response-time research, freelancer and HR surveys, documented cases. Below: the ones we hear most, in the words of the people living them, each with the exact service that fixes it.

Website & presence

if the only proof you exist is a feed

No real website, so a prospect can’t verify you’re legitimate

CO
“Someone asked for my website on a Tuesday and I sent them my Instagram. She said thanks, that’s great, and then just — nothing. I’d been telling myself the site could wait because everything came through referrals anyway. It’s the ones that don’t come through that you never hear about.”
Coach · composite account

The answerWe build the site, write it, and launch it — so the next “what’s your website?” has an answer that closes instead of stalls. → Website, built for you

SB
“The email went first. Then a client rang to say my site was showing a page of adverts. The renewal notices had been going to an address on that same domain — and the card on file had expired in January. The ten days of bounced enquiries are just gone.”
Small-business owner · composite account

The answerRenewals, DNS, and the card on file become our problem, with our own monitoring on your uptime. Your site doesn’t die of paperwork. → Hosting & domain

23–33%

SB

Marketing & visibility

what happens when the real work gets busy
51%

Posting stops the moment client work picks up — and nobody notices until the pipeline does

CO
“I checked the analytics on a Tuesday and realised the last thing I’d posted was in March. It’s May. Nothing dramatic happened — I signed two clients in April and just stopped. Enquiries kept trickling in off older stuff for a while. They’ve stopped now.”
Coach · composite account

The answerThe calendar is drafted ahead and keeps publishing precisely when you’re busiest — you approve batches, we handle the rhythm. → Social media marketing

49%

Feast-or-famine repeats because marketing only restarts once the pipeline is already empty

FR
“January and February I was turning work away. By April I was down to two clients, so I started posting and emailing again. That work landed in June — by which point I was flat out and stopped doing all of it. Fourth year running. Same shape every time.”
Freelancer · composite account

The answerThe cycle breaks when marketing stops depending on your free time. Ours doesn’t have your busy months. → Marketing & visibility

122,974

EC

The answerWe work out what’s concretely different about your practice and put it in every sentence you publish — site, bio, proposal. → Personal branding

03 /

Leads & follow-up

what the research says happens to an inbound lead
21×

A callback within two hours can still lose to one within twenty minutes

CO
“The enquiry came in at 9:40 and I rang back at half eleven. Two hours — felt fine to me. She was lovely about it. Said she’d already booked a call with someone for Thursday. Turns out she’d filled in three forms that morning before she finished her coffee.”
Coach · composite account

The answerEvery inquiry gets a real response in minutes, drafted by our system and monitored by our team — even mid-session, even on Sunday. → Lead management

42 hrs

The average business takes 42 hours to respond to its own leads

SB
“The contact form goes to an inbox that also gets invoices, newsletters, and whatever the accountant is sending. Found four quote requests sitting in there once, the oldest from Monday. Replied to all four at half nine at night. One wrote back to say thanks — they’d sorted it.”
Small-business owner · composite account

The answerLeads stop sharing an inbox with invoices. They land in one tracked place, answered while they’re warm, escalated to you only when needed. → Lead management

69%

Most small businesses track leads on a spreadsheet, or nowhere at all

SO
“Somebody messaged in November asking about corporate sessions and I replied from the train. Meant to log it that evening. Found the thread again in February — she’d followed up once and I never saw it. The spreadsheet has 214 rows, and the status column stopped being accurate around row 90.”
Studio owner · composite account

The answerEvery conversation tracked to a resolution — booked, declined, or deferred — so “I don’t know how many I’ve lost” stops being true. → Lead management

Sales & closing

what kills a deal that felt basically done
8 touches

Deals that closed needed far more follow-up than most people ever attempt

FR
“I sent two emails and decided she wasn’t interested. Found out in March she’d signed with someone else — someone who emailed her six times. Six. I sat there looking at my sent folder, and the second email was from November. It didn’t feel like giving up at the time. It felt like being polite.”
Freelancer · composite account

The answerThe follow-ups deals need, drafted and sent on schedule under your name — warm, specific, and never awkward, because they don’t rely on your nerve. → Sales & closing support

70–80%

A booked call is not the same as a call that happens

CO
“The two o’clock on Thursday is the one that gets me. I clear the hour before it, read the intake form, sit there with the link open. Then nothing — and at twelve past I send the ‘no worries, want to find another time?’ message I keep saved in my notes app, because I send it so often.”
Coach · composite account

The answerConfirmations, reminders, and reschedules run automatically around every booking — no-shows drop when nobody has to remember. → Lead management
