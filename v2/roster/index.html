Room 03 / The network · members only

A professional network without the noise.

LinkedIn was built for every industry at once, which means it was built for none of them well. The Roster is for this work only: coaches, mentors, freelancers, and the people who run service businesses — one profile each, built from the work itself.

Request an invite
You know the feeling

Why a narrower network works

A connection request from a stranger with something to sell. A profile format built for job-seekers when you’re not seeking a job — you’re running a practice. A feed where your actual peers are outnumbered fifty to one.

The Roster narrows all of it. Connections are mutual — requested and accepted, never harvested. Your profile is built from your actual work: your specialty, your offers, your credentials, your languages, with you choosing what shows. And it links to your website and your Shortlist listing, so you maintain one identity instead of five.

What’s inside

Three things a general-purpose network can’t give you

i.

A real profile

Built from your practice

  • Specialty, offers, credentials, languages — you choose what shows
  • The Verified badge, reviewed by a person, never sold
  • One identity, linked to your site and your listing
ii.

Connections that mean it

Mutual, or not at all

  • Request and accept — both sides choose
  • No cold-pitch flood; everyone came through the same door
  • Your network is people you picked, not people who found you
iii.

Referrals, both directions

The oldest channel, organized

  • Members sending business your way — and you, theirs
  • A profile worth forwarding when someone asks “know anyone?”
  • One click from a referral to your Shortlist listing

Direct messaging arrives in a later phase, on purpose. The network comes first — an inbox is only worth having once the people writing to you are worth hearing from.
