A plain answer for coaches: what’s structurally different, so you can judge it yourself.

The short version

Not an agency. Not another app. The middle ground.

A specialist runs your marketing on our platform, you approve everything, it ships under your name. That’s the whole model — and a customer of someone else described the category best: she stopped paying a social-media agency $2,000 a month — $24,000 a year — and found what she called a middle ground, “not a full-service agency, but not just a software tool either.” Her math: about $1,900 a year.

For a coach the math is sharper still. Referrals bring 55–65% of coaching clients; directories bring 8–12%. Agencies build coaches a website and disappear. Software hands you one more login — 19% of coaches invested in new tools last year, while 54% still call better platforms a priority. We run the whole layer — website, marketing, leads, follow-up — and answer for it as one team. If you find another company doing that combination, we’d genuinely like to know.

The AI question, answered first

AI makes our specialists faster. It never makes the decisions.

A founder recently wrote about firing her social-media agency: “They weren’t using AI as a tool. They were outsourcing their taste to it… I want AI to make talented people faster. I don’t want it to make mediocre work cheaper.”

She’s right, and the numbers agree. Content people merely suspect is machine-written rates 48% less trustworthy, 57% less authentic, and carries 60% less emotional connection — suspicion alone does the damage. For a coach, whose whole product is trust, that’s fatal. So nothing here ships on AI’s judgment: AI drafts. A specialist reviews. You approve. It ships as you.

It’s also why you’ll never see us lead with “AI-powered.” The product isn’t the model — it’s the named person whose job is to catch what the model gets wrong, before your clients ever see it.
