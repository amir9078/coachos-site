The measured cost of waiting

Speed to lead, in four numbers

21×more likely to convert when answered inside 5 minutes rather than 30
42hours — the average time a business takes to answer a new lead
23%of businesses never reply to a lead at all
78%of customers buy from the first business that responds

The 21× figure is from the MIT-led Lead Response Management study; the 42 hours and the one-in-four who never reply are from Harvard Business Review’s audit of 2,241 companies. And 78% of customers buy from the first business that responds. Being first is not a nice-to-have — it is most of the sale.

A lead follow-up service pays for itself on one number: a lead answered inside five minutes is 21× more likely to convert, and the average business takes 42 hours.

Asked and answered

Three questions owners ask next

How fast should a small business respond to a lead?

Inside five minutes. A lead answered within 5 minutes is 21× more likely to convert than one answered after 30 — and after five minutes, the odds of qualifying a lead drop by roughly 80%.

What does a lead follow-up service actually do?

It answers every inquiry in minutes and keeps following up until there’s an answer — booked, declined, or waiting, never lost. Every reply is drafted by the system, reviewed by a specialist, approved by you in setup, and sent under your name.

What about leads that arrive at night or on weekends?

That’s the point of the promise: no lead of yours waits past five minutes. Ever. Under your name — at 2am, on a Sunday. The Friday-afternoon lead gets a Friday-afternoon reply.
