Why am I not getting coaching clients?

The honest answer, with the numbers attached — and the exact work that changes it.

The diagnosis, said plainly

It’s usually not a lead problem

DW
“Most coaches don’t have a lead problem. They have a credibility problem… Not because you’re bad. Because you’re forgettable.”
Daniel Williams, on LinkedIn

He’s describing a crowded field. The ICF / PwC 2025 Global Coaching Study counts 122,974 professional coaches worldwide. In a field that size, being good at coaching isn’t what runs out — being visible for it is.

The numbers underneath it

Where coaching clients actually come from

55–65%of coaching clients arrive by referral
8–12%arrive through directories
82%of clients choose on demonstrated results, not certificates

Read those together and the shape of the problem appears. Referrals bring 55–65% of coaching clients; directories bring 8–12%. And 82% of clients choose on demonstrated results — showcased outcomes, not another certification. A referral only happens when someone can describe you in one sentence and point to proof under your name. If neither exists, the referrals quietly go to whoever is easier to point at.

You’re not getting coaching clients because prospects can’t find demonstrated results under your name — 82% of clients choose on exactly that, and the referrals that bring 55–65% of clients only flow to coaches people can point to.
